# Gift Guide Outreach — Contact Sheet & Playbook

_Prepared: 2026-08-13 · For: TheGiftShuffle.com_

Goal: get **TheGiftShuffle.com** mentioned, linked, reviewed, or named in rankings on gift-guide
and gift-tool publications.

---

## 0. Read this first — the strategic reframe

The starting point for this research was Listful's ["10 Best Gift Guide Sites 2025 (Reviewed)"](https://www.listful.com/blog/best-gift-guide-sites-2025).
Before working that list, understand the distinction it hides:

**TheGiftShuffle is a website/tool, not a physical product.** That matters enormously for outreach,
because the sites on that Listful list fall into two completely different buckets:

| Bucket | What they publish | Will they link to us? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Editorial gift guides** (Wirecutter, Wired, Vogue, Glamour, Strategist, BuzzFeed, Goop) | Lists of **products** with affiliate buy links | Almost never. We are a competitor for the same affiliate revenue. They link to Amazon, not to another gift-finder site. |
| **Gift-tool roundups** (Listful, GiftList, GiftX, Pickify, AI-tool directories) | Lists of **sites and apps**, head-to-head comparisons | Yes — this is literally the format. These are the winnable targets. |

So the honest ranking of effort-to-reward is:

1. **Tier 1 — gift-tool roundups & directories.** Where we can realistically get *named on a ranking*.
   Highest hit rate, lowest effort. Start here.
2. **Tier 2 — journalist-source platforms.** Get quoted as an expert source in someone else's
   holiday-shopping article. Reliable, repeatable, real editorial links.
3. **Tier 3 — the big publications.** Long-shot for a site mention. Only worth pitching with a
   **data/expert angle**, never "please link to my site." See §4 for the angle that actually works.

A note on Tier 1 economics: most of those roundups are run by **direct competitors** (Listful, GiftList,
GiftX, and Pickify all sell/operate their own gift finder and rank themselves #1). They will happily
include us as an also-ran entry — which is still a real backlink and real referral traffic — but they
will not rank us above themselves. Pitch them as "you're missing a tool from your comparison," not as
"rank me first."

| **Qwoted** | Free tier + paid | Best-regarded of the paid options. Vets experts via interview + IP tracking, which means less noise and better response rates. |

TheGiftShuffle, a gift-discovery site with [N] curated products across [N] categories" — not as a
faceless brand. Reporters quote people. Watch for requests on: holiday shopping trends, gift-giving
psychology, budget gifting, last-minute gifts, Secret Santa, affiliate/e-commerce trends.

## 5. Timing — this is the part most people get wrong

Holiday gift guide editorial calendars run **five to six months ahead**. Today is **13 August 2026**,
which means:

| Window | What's happening | Our status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **June–July** | National **print** long-lead gift guides are built. Some deadlines close mid-August. | ⛔ **Missed for 2026 print.** Diarize for **June 2027**. |
| **August–September** | Regional print roundups assemble. Long-lead online planning starts. | ⚠️ **Closing now.** Pitch this week if at all. |
| **Late Sept–November** | **Online** gift guides are written and published. This is the big one. | ✅ **Our window. Pitch Sept–Oct.** |
| **Year-round** | Tool roundups, directories, source platforms — no seasonal deadline. | ✅ **Start immediately.** |

The good news: nearly everything in Tier 1 and Tier 2 is season-independent. The print deadline we've
missed is also the tier least likely to link to us anyway.

## 6. Before sending a single email — fix these three things

Every one of these is a reason an editor silently deletes a pitch. All three are cheap.

1. **There is no contact page and no press page on the site.** The footer links only to `/`, `/about`,
   `/privacy`, `/terms`, `/shuffle`, `/wishlist`. A journalist who wants to verify us has nowhere to go.
   Add `/press` with: one-paragraph description, founder name + bio + photo, logo assets, site stats,
   a direct email, and 2–3 pre-written "facts about gift shopping" pulled from our click data.
2. **`/about` says "Last Updated: March 2025"** and refers to "our editorial team" without naming
   anyone. Anonymous sites do not get cited. Put a real name and face on it — that alone is often the
   difference between being quoted and being ignored.
3. **Decide the honest one-line positioning** and use it everywhere. Suggested, based on what the site
   actually does: *"A free gift finder that returns a real, buyable product in under five seconds — no
   signup, no quiz, every pick vetted at 4.3★ and 1,000+ reviews."* That last clause is a genuine,
   checkable editorial standard, and it's more compelling than "AI-powered," which every competitor
   claims.

## 7. Pitch templates

### 7a. Tier 1 — link insertion into an existing roundup

> **Subject:** Missing from your 2026 gift finder comparison
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I read your [exact article title] — the [specific, genuine detail you actually noticed] point
> matched what we see in our own data.
>
> One tool that isn't in the comparison: **TheGiftShuffle** (thegiftshuffle.com). Free, no signup,
> returns a real in-stock product with a direct buy link in about five seconds. The differentiator
> vs. the generative tools you tested: every product is hand-vetted against a published standard
> (4.3★ minimum, 1,000+ reviews), so it never returns something that doesn't exist.
>
> Takes 30 seconds to test if you want to check the claim. Happy to send whatever you'd need for an
> entry — screenshots, category counts, whatever format you use.
>
> [Name], founder, TheGiftShuffle

### 7b. Tier 3 — data pitch to a major publication

> **Subject:** Gift-search data for your holiday coverage — [N] searches, [surprising finding]
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I run TheGiftShuffle, a free gift-finder site. We've logged [N] gift searches and [N] outbound
> purchase clicks this year, broken out by recipient, budget tier, and occasion.
>
> Three things in the data I haven't seen reported anywhere:
> 1. [specific, quantified, counterintuitive]
> 2. [specific, quantified, counterintuitive]
> 3. [specific, quantified, counterintuitive]
>
> If any of it is useful for your holiday coverage, the full breakdown is yours — and I'm available
> for a quote on [gifting trend] on your timeline.
>
> [Name], founder, TheGiftShuffle

**Rules for both:** under 150 words. Subject line states the value, not the ask. Reference something
specific about their actual work — the generic-blast pitch is instantly recognisable and instantly
deleted. One follow-up after 7–10 days, then stop.
